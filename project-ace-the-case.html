// WHY THIS NEEDED SOLVING

IndoMart, a global retail giant, is planning to acquire GangaBazaar, a leading Indian volume retailer — a simulated deal that would reshape the competitive landscape overnight. The case asked us to assess how much of a threat this poses to BharatMart, a mid-tier Indian retail challenger caught between the two.

It wasn't enough to say "this is bad for BharatMart." The brief demanded a real financial teardown of all three companies, a regulatory read on how fast the deal could realistically close, and — critically — a concrete, numbers-backed counter-strategy BharatMart could actually execute.

// HOW WE BUILT THE CASE
  • Financial teardown first: sized all three players on revenue, profit margin, and store count before proposing anything, to ground the threat in real numbers rather than assumption.
  • Regulatory lens: mapped FDI caps on multi-brand retail and likely CCI antitrust scrutiny to judge how much runway BharatMart actually has before the merger closes.
  • Guesstimate modeling: built bottom-up revenue models for two separate counter-strategies — Kirana network partnerships and apparel customization — rather than leaving them as narrative ideas.
  • SWOT framing: stress-tested the final recommendation against BharatMart's actual strengths and weaknesses, not a generic template.
// THE RECOMMENDATION

Turn India's dense Kirana (local grocer) network into BharatMart's structural advantage — using ~2,500 local stores as micro-fulfillment hubs and franchise partners instead of competing head-on with IndoMart's discount-volume model.

Our model showed current delivery costs eating roughly 52% of online order value. A 66/34 revenue-split partnership with local vendors flipped that ratio, projecting online revenue rising from ₹67.6cr to ₹93.9cr in the model — alongside a secondary apparel-customization play projected to add ₹158cr+ in incremental revenue.

Six workstreams stress-tested across the case, not just the headline number.

Comparative Teardown

Sized IndoMart, GangaBazaar, and BharatMart on revenue, margin, and store footprint before proposing any response.

02 — REGULATORY

FDI & Antitrust Read

Mapped multi-brand retail FDI caps and CCI scrutiny to judge how much time BharatMart realistically has.

03 — DISTRIBUTION

Kirana Network Model

Modeled a 66/34 revenue-split partnership turning 2,500 local stores into fulfillment and franchise hubs.

04 — APPAREL

Customization Pricing

Sized the 18–29 demographic and modeled tiered customization pricing for a projected ₹158cr+ uplift.

05 — INVENTORY

Dead Stock Recovery

Cross-category bundling model to recover ~15% of otherwise sunk unsold inventory cost.

06 — POLITICAL

Domestic Positioning

Framed BharatMart's domestic-first narrative against Atmanirbhar Bharat priorities as regulatory leverage.
