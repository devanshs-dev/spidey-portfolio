Ace The Case 2.0

The Brief

A simulated retail-acquisition scenario: US giant IndoMart plans to acquire GangaBazaar, an Indian volume retailer. The case asked us to assess the threat this poses to BharatMart — India's mid-tier retail challenger — and build a defensible strategic response, backed by financial modeling and market sizing.

Financial & Market Analysis

We opened with a comparative financial teardown of all three players — IndoMart's ~$611B global revenue against BharatMart's ₹88,522cr and GangaBazaar's ₹41,833cr — to size the actual competitive gap before proposing anything. From there we built a regulatory read (FDI caps on multi-brand retail, CCI antitrust scrutiny) to judge how fast IndoMart could realistically move.

The Core Recommendation: Kirana Network Play

Our central strategy was using India's dense Kirana (local grocer) network as BharatMart's counter-move — turning ~2,500 local stores into micro-fulfillment hubs and franchise partners instead of competing purely on IndoMart's discount-volume model. We modeled the delivery-cost economics directly: current shipment costs were eating ~52% of online order value; a 66/34 revenue-split partnership with local vendors flipped that, pushing projected online revenue from ₹67.6cr to ₹93.9cr per the model.

Supporting Strategies

  • Apparel customization: Sized the 18–29 demographic (BharatMart's dominant apparel segment) and modeled a tiered customization pricing structure, projecting a ~₹158cr revenue lift over the flat-price baseline.
  • Unsold inventory recovery: Proposed a cross-category bundling model (e.g. cosmetics with apparel, packaged food with beverages) to move dead stock — recovering roughly 15% of otherwise sunk inventory cost.
  • Regulatory positioning: Framed BharatMart's domestic-first narrative against Atmanirbhar Bharat policy priorities, as leverage in the event of political or regulatory pushback on the IndoMart deal.

Leading the Team

As Team Lead, my role was less about the case content itself and more about process — splitting the workload across financial modeling, market research, design, and content within a tight turnaround, keeping seven people aligned on one narrative, and resolving direction disagreements fast enough that they didn't stall the team. Roles were split across business analysis, research, market sizing, design (two co-leads), and content — with the final deck pulled together under my direction to read as one coherent argument rather than seven disconnected sections.

What I Took From It

Running point on a team this size under competition constraints reinforced something I'd apply to any project since: the hardest part of a group deliverable usually isn't the analysis, it's the coordination. Getting seven people to converge on one storyline, under pressure, with fast decisions, mattered more than any single number in the model.
